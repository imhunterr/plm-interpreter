Method

How PLM Interpreter works

One question: when ESM-2 says a mutation is bad, why does it say so, and can we trust that explanation?

  1. 1

    Hide and guess

    ESM-2 was trained to fill in hidden amino acids. To judge a mutation we hide that position and compare how likely the new amino acid is with the original.

    score = log P(new) − log P(original)
  2. 2

    Check the score is right

    Our scores reproduce the published ProteinGym benchmark in all 217 experiments, so every explanation is about the real model.

  3. 3

    Explain at three levels

    Residues that drove the score, attention heads it depends on, and layers where structure and the verdict appear.

  4. 4

    Test against reality

    Explanations are compared with AlphaFold2 structures, conservation across species and lab measurements, always alongside an untrained model as a control.

ESM-2
A protein language model from Meta. We use the 8-million and 35-million parameter versions, which run on a laptop.
Mask
Hiding one amino acid so the model has to guess it.
Score (LLR)
How much less (or more) likely the new amino acid is than the original, on a log scale. −4 means about 55× less likely.
Layer
One processing stage. Information passes through 6 (8M) or 12 (35M) layers.
Attention head
A small unit inside a layer that decides which other positions to look at. 35M has 240, 8M has 120.
Integrated gradients
A standard way to give each input residue a share of the model's output.
Ablation
Switching part of the model off and measuring what changes.
Logit lens
Reading the model's guess after each layer instead of only at the end.
DMS
Deep mutational scanning: a lab experiment that measures thousands of mutations of one protein.
Spearman
Correlation from −1 to 1: do two lists rank items in the same order?
AUROC
How well a score separates two groups. 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect.
