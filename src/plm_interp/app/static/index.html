The model reads a protein as letters

A protein is a chain of amino acids, each written as one letter. ESM-2 learned from millions of natural proteins by playing one game: hide a letter and guess it back. Click any residue to hide it.

The model's guess

Score the mutation

Pick a replacement amino acid in step 3.

The score compares how likely the model finds the new amino acid with the original one: log P(new) − log P(original). Below zero means "less natural than the original". Very negative usually means damaging.

What we found

Eight results from running the full pipeline on ESM-2 8M and 35M, 217 lab experiments, 62,727 clinical variants and 60 protein structures. Every result is compared with a control: usually an untrained copy of the same model.

How it works

The project asks one question: when ESM-2 says a mutation is bad, why does it say so, and can we trust that explanation?

01

Hide and guess

ESM-2 was trained to fill in hidden amino acids. To judge a mutation we hide that position and ask how likely the new amino acid is compared with the original.

score = log P(new) − log P(original)

02

Check the score is right

Our scores reproduce the published ProteinGym benchmark for all 217 experiments, so every explanation is about the real model behaviour.

03

Explain at three levels

Residues: which other amino acids drove the score. Components: which attention heads it depends on. Layers: what each layer knows, and where the decision forms.

04

Test against reality

Explanations are compared with 3D structures (AlphaFold2), conservation across species and lab measurements, always alongside an untrained model as a control.

ESM-2
A protein language model from Meta. We use the 8-million and 35-million parameter versions, which run on a laptop.
Mask
Hiding one amino acid so the model has to guess it.
Score (LLR)
Log-likelihood ratio: how much less (or more) likely the new amino acid is than the original. −4 means about 55× less likely.
Layer
One processing stage. Information passes through 6 (8M) or 12 (35M) layers.
Attention head
A small unit inside a layer that decides which other positions to look at. 35M has 240, 8M has 120.
Integrated gradients
A standard way to assign each input residue a share of the model's output.
Ablation
Switching a part of the model off and measuring what breaks.
Logit lens
Reading the model's guess after each layer instead of only at the end.
DMS
Deep mutational scanning: a lab experiment that measures thousands of mutations of one protein.
Spearman
Correlation from −1 to 1: do two lists rank items in the same order?
AUROC
How well a score separates two groups. 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect.
